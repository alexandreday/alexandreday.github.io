Reinforcement learning in different phases of quantum control

Marin Bukov, Alexandre G.R. Day, Dries Sels, Phillip Weinberg, Anatoli Polkovnikov, Pankaj Mehta

Boston University · 2018–2019

Phys. Rev. X Phys. Rev. Lett. Phys. Rev. A
TL;DR

Guided by reinforcement learning (Q-learning) and stochastic descent, we map out the optimization landscape of quantum state preparation and show that it undergoes phase transitions: as the constraints on the controls change, the landscape goes from smooth and convex to rugged and glassy, with direct consequences for how hard the control problem is.

The problem

Quantum control consists in tuning in real time a set of knobs which set the intensity of some interactions or couplings of a control Hamiltonian. The control Hamiltonian dictates the dynamical state of a quantum state and, as the controls are changed, the quantum state evolves following Schrödinger's dynamics. Ultimately, the goal of quantum control is to prepare certain quantum states with desirable properties (an entangled state, for instance). This is of fundamental interest for cold-atom systems, quantum computing and many “hot” fields of quantum sciences.

What we found

In our work we explore the different geometries that the control landscape can take as different constraints are imposed on the system. We show in particular that quantum control undergoes multiple phase transitions from smooth phases to rugged and glassy phases. Our exploration is guided by reinforcement learning methods along with stochastic descent optimization.
